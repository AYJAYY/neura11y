# Australia Accessibility Laws Overview

**IMPORTANT:** Legal information changes. Verify with current official sources. Last reviewed: 2026-03-13.

---

## Disability Discrimination Act (DDA) 1992

The **Disability Discrimination Act 1992** is Australia's primary federal anti-discrimination legislation.

### Scope

Applies to:
- All public and private sector organisations operating in Australia
- Covers goods, services, facilities, accommodation, employment, education
- Includes access to websites and digital services as "services"

### Digital Accessibility Application

The DDA does not explicitly name web standards, but the **Australian Human Rights Commission (AHRC)** has issued advisory notes treating inaccessible websites as potential discrimination under the DDA.

**AHRC position:** Failure to make a website reasonably accessible to people with disability may constitute unlawful discrimination under the DDA.

**Key case — Maguire v SOCOG (2000):** The Sydney Olympics website was found to have breached the DDA by failing to provide accessible content for a blind user. This was the world's first successful legal complaint about web accessibility.

## Web Content Accessibility Guidelines — Commonwealth Agencies

### Australian Government Mandatory Standard

**AGMO (Australian Government Mandatory Requirements):** Under the **Digital Service Standard** issued by the Digital Transformation Agency (DTA), Australian Government agencies must:

- Meet **WCAG 2.0 Level AA** as a minimum requirement
- Aim for **WCAG 2.1 Level AA** for new and redesigned services
- Test with actual users including people with disability

**Applies to:**
- Commonwealth (federal) government agencies
- Government digital services and websites

### State and Territory Governments

Most state and territory governments have adopted comparable requirements, typically referencing WCAG 2.0 or 2.1 Level AA.

| Jurisdiction | Reference Standard | Notes |
|-------------|-------------------|-------|
| Commonwealth | WCAG 2.0/2.1 AA | Digital Service Standard, DTA |
| NSW | WCAG 2.0 AA | NSW Government Digital Strategy |
| Victoria | WCAG 2.1 AA | Victorian Government guidance |
| Queensland | WCAG 2.1 AA | QLD Government requirements |
| Western Australia | WCAG 2.0 AA | WA Digital Standards |
| South Australia | WCAG 2.0 AA | SA ICT guidance |

## Australian Human Rights Commission (AHRC)

The AHRC is the primary body for DDA complaints and guidance.

### Complaint Process

1. **Conciliation first:** AHRC attempts conciliation between the complainant and organisation
2. **Federal Court:** If conciliation fails, complainant can escalate to the Federal Court of Australia
3. **Remedies:** May include apologies, access plans, financial compensation

### AHRC Advisory Notes

The AHRC has published guidance on web accessibility and the DDA:
- Advisory Note 2010: "Accessibility of the World Wide Web"
- Position: Websites that exclude people with disability may constitute unlawful discrimination
- Recommends WCAG compliance as evidence of reasonable adjustment

## Practical Requirements for Organisations

### Who Should Comply

**Government agencies (Commonwealth):** Mandatory — Digital Service Standard applies.

**State/Territory agencies:** Strong guidance and policy requirements; DDA exposure for non-compliance.

**Private sector:** No explicit mandate, but DDA exposure if websites are inaccessible. Risk increases with:
- Size of organisation
- Nature of service (essential services, banking, healthcare, utilities)
- Knowledge of accessibility issues

### Recommended Standard

**Best practice for Australia:** WCAG 2.1 Level AA

- Exceeds the Commonwealth mandatory minimum (WCAG 2.0 AA)
- Aligns with international standards (EU, UK, US)
- Provides strong defense against DDA complaints

## National Disability Strategy

Australia's **National Disability Strategy 2021–2031** provides a broader policy framework. It emphasises:
- Inclusive design of digital services
- Participation of people with disability in digital economy
- Government accountability for accessible services

The strategy is a policy document; enforcement relies on the DDA and individual agency standards.
