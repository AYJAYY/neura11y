# UK Accessibility Regulations Overview

**IMPORTANT:** Legal information changes. Verify with current UK government sources. Last reviewed: 2026-03-13.

---

## Public Sector: The Accessibility Regulations 2018

The **Public Sector Bodies (Websites and Mobile Applications) (No. 2) Accessibility Regulations 2018** (SI 2018/952) implement the EU Web Accessibility Directive in UK law. Post-Brexit, this regulation continues to apply independently in the UK.

### Scope

Applies to public sector bodies including:
- Central government departments and agencies
- Local authorities
- NHS trusts and health bodies
- Educational institutions (schools, universities)
- Other public sector bodies

### Technical Standard

Requires conformance with **WCAG 2.1 Level AA**.

### Requirements

1. **Meet WCAG 2.1 AA** for websites and mobile apps
2. **Publish an accessibility statement** on each website/app:
   - State compliance level
   - List known non-compliant content
   - Provide contact details for reporting issues
   - Provide link to enforcement body
3. **Respond to users' requests** for accessible alternative formats within reasonable time
4. **Update statements** when content changes

### Exemptions

- Third-party content the body doesn't control or fund
- Live audio/video
- Online maps (where essential information is provided in alternative accessible format)
- Content produced by other public bodies
- Archived content (before September 23, 2019) unless needed for active administrative process

### Enforcement

Monitored by the **Central Digital and Data Office (CDDO)** — formerly the Government Digital Service (GDS). Cabinet Office.

Enforcement mechanism: If a user complains to the body and doesn't get a satisfactory response, they can escalate to the **Equality and Human Rights Commission (EHRC)**.

## Accessibility Statement Template (UK GOV)

The UK Government provides an accessibility statement template and generator:
- Statement must use the UK accessibility regulations template format
- Must be published at a predictable location (e.g., `/accessibility`)
- Must be updated when new content is published or issues found

Example required content:
```
[Organisation name] is committed to making its website accessible...
This website is fully / partially / not compliant with WCAG 2.1 AA
[List of known issues and reasons]
[Date last reviewed]
[Reporting mechanism]
[Enforcement procedure link]
```

## Private Sector: Equality Act 2010

For private sector organizations, the **Equality Act 2010** applies. It requires that service providers make "reasonable adjustments" to avoid placing disabled people at a "substantial disadvantage."

While the Equality Act does not explicitly mandate WCAG compliance, case law and guidance (from the EHRC) treats WCAG 2.1 AA as the recognized standard for digital accessibility.

**Key principle:** An inaccessible website may constitute failure to make a "reasonable adjustment," creating legal exposure under the Equality Act.

---

## UK Accessibility Act (Analogous to EU EAA)

Following Brexit, the UK is expected to implement its own digital accessibility requirements for the private sector, analogous to the EU European Accessibility Act. The UK Accessibility Act — sometimes referred to in consultation documents — would extend requirements to private sector products and services.

**Status as of 2026:** In development/consultation. Timelines differ from the EU EAA (which requires private sector compliance from June 28, 2025). Check current UK government publications for latest status.
