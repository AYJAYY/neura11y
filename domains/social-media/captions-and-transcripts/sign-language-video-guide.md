# Sign Language for Social Video

---

## Important Scope Note

Under WCAG, sign language for prerecorded synchronized media is SC 1.2.6 at Level AAA, not Level AA.

That means:

- It is not part of a basic WCAG 2.2 AA baseline
- It may still be the right product, audience, or legal choice
- Captions remain necessary for many users even when sign language is provided

---

## Sign Language Is Not a Caption Replacement

Do not treat sign language interpretation as a substitute for captions.

### Why

- Not all deaf or hard-of-hearing users use sign language
- ASL, BSL, and other sign languages are distinct languages, not signed English subtitles
- Captions provide exact text, speaker ID, and sound cues

### Better Pattern

For audience-critical video:

- Provide captions
- Provide sign language when the audience or service context warrants it
- Provide a transcript when reuse or searchability matters

---

## When Sign Language Is Worth Adding

Sign language support is especially high-value when:

- The primary audience includes sign-first Deaf users
- The content concerns rights, services, safety, healthcare, education, or legal obligations
- The video uses complex spoken language that plain captions may not fully bridge
- The organization already serves a strong ASL, BSL, or local-sign-language community

---

## Delivery Patterns

| Pattern | Best Use | Risks |
|---------|----------|-------|
| Picture-in-picture interpreter | Short announcements and explainers | Interpreter window can be too small or blocked |
| Separate sign-language version | Long-form content or platform republishing | Requires clear linking between versions |
| Sign-first video with captions | Community-first communication | Spoken-language users may still need narration or transcript |

---

## Interpreter Window Guidance

If using a picture-in-picture interpreter:

- Keep the signer large enough for hand shape and facial expression to remain clear
- Do not cover the interpreter with captions, stickers, or platform chrome
- Use a background and clothing contrast that keeps hands and face visible
- Avoid jump cuts that crop the interpreter unpredictably
- Keep lighting consistent

### Common Failure

- Interpreter is squeezed into a corner
- Auto-captions sit directly on top of signing space
- Animated overlays or reaction badges obscure the hands

---

## Caption Coordination

Sign language and captions must be designed together.

### Better Pattern

- Reserve a caption-safe area separate from the interpreter window
- Burned-in text should not overlap the signer
- If the platform adds captions automatically, verify the final posted layout

### Production Rule

Check the rendered video in the actual mobile app, not only in the editing timeline.

---

## Sign-First Video Guidance

For sign-first content:

- Identify the sign language in the post copy when relevant
- Add captions for spoken audio and signed meaning as needed for the intended audience
- Provide voiceover or transcript if the message also needs to reach non-signing audiences

### Example Post Note

`ASL version available`

or

`This video is presented in BSL with captions`

This sets expectations clearly before playback.

---

## Writing Requirements Clearly

Avoid vague requests like:

- `Add subtitles`
- `Make it accessible`

Use explicit requests like:

- `Provide closed captions with speaker identification and sound cues`
- `Create an ASL-interpreted version with captions that do not cover the signer`
- `Publish a transcript in the post body or linked page`

---

## Review Checklist

- [ ] Captions are present and accurate
- [ ] Sign language is identified correctly for the audience
- [ ] Interpreter window is large enough and unobstructed
- [ ] Captions do not overlap the signing space
- [ ] Transcript is available when long-term reuse matters
- [ ] The posted platform version was checked on mobile
