# Accessible Emoji Guide

Emoji are announced by screen readers using their Unicode names. Overuse or awkward placement can make content noisy and slow to understand.

---

## Core Principles

- Use emoji sparingly
- Do not replace important words with emoji alone
- Put emoji after the relevant text, not before it
- Avoid long emoji strings
- Test whether the spoken emoji names help or distract

---

## Better Patterns

| Less Accessible | Better |
|-----------------|--------|
| `🔥🔥🔥 New release` | `New release 🔥` |
| `Click the 📧` | `Email us` |
| `✅❌` without text | `Approved ✅` / `Rejected ❌` |

---

## Why Placement Matters

Screen readers announce the emoji name inline. Leading emoji can make the user hear decorative content before the actual message.

Example:

- Less accessible: `🎉 Launch day is here`
- Better: `Launch day is here 🎉`

---

## When Emoji Are Helpful

- adding light tone after the main message
- reinforcing a short label that already exists in text
- casual social content where the spoken emoji name still makes sense

Emoji should supplement meaning, not carry it alone.
