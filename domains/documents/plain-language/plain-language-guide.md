# Plain Language Guide

Source: Federal Plain Language Guidelines (plainlanguage.gov), adapted with COGA guidance.

---

## Why Plain Language for Accessibility

Plain language directly addresses cognitive accessibility requirements:
- WCAG SC 3.1.5 (Reading Level, AAA): content at lower secondary education level or supplemental simpler version
- COGA Objective 3: Use clear and understandable content
- Section 508: plain language requirements for federal communications
- Broad benefit: clear writing helps everyone, especially people with cognitive disabilities, learning disabilities, non-native speakers, and those under stress or time pressure

## Words

### Use Common, Everyday Words

| Complex word | Plain alternative |
|-------------|------------------|
| Utilize | Use |
| Commence | Start, begin |
| Terminate | End, stop |
| Facilitate | Help, make possible |
| Endeavor | Try |
| Approximately | About |
| Subsequent to | After |
| Prior to | Before |
| Ascertain | Find out |
| Demonstrate | Show |
| Obtain | Get |
| Provide | Give |
| Require | Need |
| Sufficient | Enough |
| Indicate | Show, tell |
| Purchase | Buy |
| Residence | Home |

### Define Technical Terms

On first use, define terms in plain language:

```
The Voluntary Product Accessibility Template (VPAT) is a document
companies use to describe how their product meets accessibility requirements.
```

After definition, you may use the acronym or term.

### Avoid Jargon Unless Necessary

**Government/legal jargon examples and replacements:**

| Jargon | Plain alternative |
|--------|-----------------|
| Pursuant to | Under, according to |
| In lieu of | Instead of |
| In accordance with | Under, following |
| Notwithstanding | Even though, despite |
| Hereinafter | From now on |
| Aforementioned | The, this |
| Said [noun] | The [noun] |

### Consistent Terminology

Use the same word for the same concept throughout. Don't alternate synonyms:
- Consistent: "button" throughout
- Inconsistent: "button," "control," "element," "widget" for the same thing

## Sentences

### Active Voice

Active voice: subject does the action.
Passive voice: subject receives the action.

| Passive | Active |
|---------|--------|
| A confirmation will be sent to you | We will send you a confirmation |
| Mistakes were made | We made mistakes |
| The form must be completed | You must complete the form |
| Errors are highlighted in red | Red highlighting shows errors |
| Eligibility will be determined | We will determine eligibility |

### Short Sentences

Target: 15–20 words maximum per sentence.

**Long sentence (31 words):**
> The committee will review all submitted applications and determine eligibility based on the criteria established in the program guidelines and notify all applicants of the decision within 30 days.

**Broken into short sentences (two sentences, avg. 16 words):**
> The committee will review all applications and determine eligibility based on the program guidelines. We will notify all applicants of the decision within 30 days.

### One Idea per Sentence

Avoid complex sentences with multiple clauses.

**Complex:**
> While applicants who submitted their forms before the deadline may be eligible for the early review process, those who submitted late will need to wait until the next review cycle, which begins in April.

**Simple:**
> Applicants who submitted before the deadline may qualify for early review. Late applicants will be reviewed in the next cycle, starting in April.

### Avoid Double Negatives

| Double negative | Positive version |
|----------------|-----------------|
| Not uncommon | Common |
| Not ineligible | Eligible |
| Not without merit | Has merit |
| Not impossible | Possible |
| No person shall not | Every person shall |

## Paragraphs

### One Topic per Paragraph

Each paragraph should cover one main idea. If a paragraph covers two topics, split it.

### Short Paragraphs

Target: 3–5 sentences maximum for body text. Shorter for web content.

### Inverted Pyramid

Lead with the most important information. Details and exceptions follow.

**Legal writing pattern (inverted pyramid violated):**
> Under Section 5 of the regulations as amended in 2019, subject to the exceptions enumerated in subsection (b) and (c), and provided that the applicant has maintained continuous residency for the period specified in the eligibility guidelines, applications submitted in the prescribed format on or before the stated deadline will be considered.

**Plain language version:**
> Apply by [date] to be considered. You must have lived in the area continuously for [period]. See Section 5 for exceptions.

## Structure

### Headings

Good headings:
- Describe what follows, not just a label
- Use question form to engage readers: "How do I reset my password?"
- Use action form: "Reset your password" vs. "Password reset"
- Are specific: "How to appeal a decision" vs. "Appeals"

### Lists

Use numbered lists for:
- Sequential steps (do this first, then this)
- Ranked items
- Procedures where order matters

Use bulleted lists for:
- Items without a required order
- Features, benefits, options
- Examples

**Rule of three:** If you have three or more items in a sentence, convert to a list.

**Sentence form:**
> You will need your passport, driver's license, social security card, and utility bill.

**List form:**
> You will need:
> - Passport
> - Driver's license
> - Social security card
> - Utility bill

### Tables

Use tables to compare items with multiple attributes. Don't use tables for simple lists.

**Not a table:** A list of five steps in a process.
**Good table:** A comparison of three pricing plans with six features each.

## Plain Language for Web Content

Web readers scan before reading. Additional web-specific guidelines:

- **Put the most important content first** — users may not scroll
- **Use descriptive link text** — "Read the 2025 Annual Report" not "Read more"
- **Keep paragraphs shorter** — 2–3 sentences max on web
- **Break text with headers every 150–200 words** on web
- **Use bold for key terms** — helps scanners find important information
- **Avoid scrolling text, carousels for critical information** — missed by scanners

## Plain Language Checklist

- [ ] Sentences average under 20 words
- [ ] Active voice (verify less than 25% passive)
- [ ] No undefined jargon or acronyms
- [ ] Common words used (utilize → use, commence → start)
- [ ] One idea per sentence
- [ ] Short paragraphs (3–5 sentences)
- [ ] Most important information first
- [ ] Lists for 3+ items
- [ ] Descriptive headings
- [ ] Reading level tested (target Grade 6–8 for general audiences)
