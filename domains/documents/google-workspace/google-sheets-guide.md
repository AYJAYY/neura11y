# Google Sheets Accessibility Guide

## Worksheet Structure

- Put the data table at the top-left of the sheet
- Use one clear header row
- Freeze header rows when the sheet is long
- Avoid blank rows and columns inside the primary data region

## Labels and Instructions

- Give each worksheet a meaningful tab name
- Explain abbreviations, formulas, and status colors in visible text
- Use notes or a separate instruction sheet when the workbook has several workflows

## Charts and Conditional Formatting

- Add chart titles that explain the key point, not just the metric name
- Use direct labels or legends with strong contrast
- Do not rely on conditional formatting color alone to communicate pass/fail or priority

## Sharing and Export

- Check filter views and protected ranges for keyboard access
- Re-test charts and tables after exporting to Excel or PDF
- If the spreadsheet is a reporting surface, consider a companion narrative summary
